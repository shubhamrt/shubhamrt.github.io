Mongolia Rangeland Watch

Evidence before grazing.

A research prototype combining current satellite vegetation observations with recent moisture conditions and Mongolia’s recovery-class framework to support more transparent rangeland decisions.

What this site does not doIt does not create legal grazing bans and it does not infer carrying capacity from NDVI alone. Official closures must come from competent Mongolian authorities. Stocking-rate estimates will only be added after a validated biomass calibration is available.

Decision logic

The prototype separates current forage signal from ecological recovery need. That prevents a temporarily green but degraded pasture from automatically being labelled suitable for grazing.

  1. Find the newest usable Sentinel-2 scene. Cloud, snow, water and shadow pixels are rejected using the Sentinel-2 Scene Classification Layer.
  2. Calculate local NDVI. Red and near-infrared surface reflectance are sampled at the selected point. Reflectance scaling is applied by the data service.
  3. Compare like with like. The current NDVI is compared with valid observations from approximately the same time of year over up to five previous years. This is more meaningful across Mongolia than one national NDVI threshold.
  4. Add current environmental context. Recent precipitation, reference evapotranspiration and modelled near-surface soil moisture are reported, but are not presented as satellite measurements.
  5. Apply the recovery constraint. If a recovery class is known, it takes priority over temporary greenness. Class IV–V areas are treated as restoration/rest priorities; classes II–III trigger progressively more conservative grazing advice.
  6. Keep legal status separate. A future official closure layer can override all ecological screening. Until an authoritative closure feed is connected, the site never labels a location “legally closed.”
