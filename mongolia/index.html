Should livestock graze here?

A live, evidence-based recommendation combining current vegetation condition, terrain, soil erodibility, recent moisture and Mongolia's rangeland recovery framework.

Decision-support, not lawThe site now gives an explicit GRAZE, GRAZE CAUTIOUSLY or DO NOT GRAZE / REST recommendation. It does not create a legal closure. Recommendations are provisional until validated against Mongolian field and grazing-outcome data.

Decision logic

The model treats forage condition, ecological recovery and erosion vulnerability as separate constraints. A green site can still receive a REST recommendation if terrain or soil makes loss of protective cover risky.

Code noteValidation roadmap and limitations
  1. Current vegetation. A valid Sentinel-2 red/NIR pixel is screened for cloud, snow, water and shadow. NDVI is compared with up to five same-season prior years.
  2. Terrain. Four neighbouring Copernicus DEM samples are used to estimate local slope at approximately the 30 m terrain scale.
  3. Surface soil. SoilGrids 0–5 cm sand, silt, clay and organic carbon are queried at their native ~250 m scale. USDA texture is derived from the fractions.
  4. Erodibility. A soil-erodibility term is estimated from texture and organic carbon using the EPIC/Sharpley–Williams formulation. This is combined with slope and vegetation protection to screen water-erosion susceptibility. A separate aeolian-exposure screen uses surface texture, vegetation protection and recent wind.
  5. Recommendation. The dominant erosion pathway, vegetation stress, moisture context and any supplied recovery class are combined. Recovery Classes IV–V are hard restoration/rest constraints.
  6. Resolution is explicit. The finest input is 10 m, but a soil-informed decision cannot honestly claim 10 m soil precision. The page reports all native resolutions and confidence instead of resampling 250 m soil information and calling it finer.
