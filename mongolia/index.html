Mongolia Rangeland Watch

Where should livestock graze?

A transparent decision-support prototype combining 10 m land cover and vegetation, terrain, surface soil, erosion susceptibility and recovery status. Pastures can also be drawn to calculate provisional herd capacity.

The published Mongolia analysis maps rangeland-condition change at 30 m and reports higher decline evidence in intensively grazed central areas. It also separates rainfall variability from management effects. I am keeping this distinct from today's grazing recommendation.

What changed

The decision chain now starts with land-use eligibility. This prevents an urban lawn, irrigated crop or water body from entering a rangeland suitability calculation. Pasture capacity is kept at management-unit scale.

10 mESA WorldCover · land-cover eligibility
10 mSentinel-2 · current vegetation
30 mCopernicus DEM · terrain
250 mSoilGrids · surface soil
  1. Eligibility first. Built-up, cropland, water, snow/ice and tree cover stop the model before a grazing score is produced.
  2. Current condition. Eligible rangeland is evaluated against its own same-season Sentinel-2 history.
  3. Degradation risk. Slope, mapped soil erodibility, vegetation protection and wind/moisture context contribute to separate water and wind erosion screens.
  4. Capacity. A drawn pasture uses supplied biomass, 1.4 kg DM per Mongolian Sheep Unit per day and a 30% baseline harvest efficiency. Cautious/rest conditions reduce that amount.
  5. Historical stress stays separate. A decline map is not labelled grazing-caused unless climatic variability and management pressure have been addressed explicitly.
