What London Forgot to Learn from Rain

Living with scarcity is a skill. We are all going to need it.

The first heavy rain I witnessed in Las Cruces, New Mexico drew people outside to watch it. A neighbor held out her hand to feel it. Someone photographed the arroyos filling. The smell of rain on desert soil rose from streets that had been dry for months. I had moved from London, where rain falls more than twice as often and no one looks up. In England, rain is background. In New Mexico, it is an event. The difference is not only meteorological. It is civilizational.

The Ground Beneath Her Feet

On land tenure, soil recovery, and why the two problems cannot be solved separately

In the Banni grasslands of Kutch, in western India, the Maldhari people have grazed cattle for centuries on land they do not legally own. The state classifies it as wasteland. The Maldharis know it as something else entirely: a living system of seasonal routes, water sources and communal memory that has sustained their herds longer than the Indian state has existed.
